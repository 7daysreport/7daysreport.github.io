How the weekly boards are built

A ranking, not a buy list. Every board answers one narrow question with public data, shows its dates, and says what it can't tell you.

Last updated: November 8, 2026 · Applies to The Weekly Board (Shorts) and The 2-Minute Check (videos).

Education only, not investment advice. We don't pick stocks, give price targets or tell anyone to buy or sell. A low ratio is a question to research, not an answer.

1. What we rank

Each week one Short ranks one factor across the same universe. A paired 2-minute video re-checks that Top 10 with two companion factors that catch the factor's usual trap.

BoardFormulaOrder2-minute check (both must pass)
Lowest P/BMarket value ÷ shareholders' equityLowest firstROE ≥ 10% · Liabilities ÷ equity ≤ 2.0
Lowest P/EMarket value ÷ net income, trailing four quartersLowest firstRevenue growth (year over year) > 0% · Free cash flow ≥ 70% of net income
Highest FCF yield(Operating cash flow − capital expenditure), trailing four quarters ÷ market valueHighest firstRevenue growth > 0% · Liabilities ÷ equity ≤ 2.0

The pass thresholds are common rules of thumb, published so you can disagree with them. They are our filter, not a correct answer. ROE is used only as a companion check: on its own it is distorted by buybacks that shrink equity toward zero.

2. Universe

  • The 500 largest US-listed companies by market value, counted by us each week (last sale price × shares outstanding from the company's latest SEC filing cover). We do not use any licensed index membership list.
  • Excluded from every board: banks, insurers, REITs and other financial companies (SEC industry codes 6000–6799), whose balance sheets make these ratios misleading; companies with more than one share class (one price can't value them); companies whose latest reported quarter ended before June 30, 2026; foreign filers without quarterly US reports; stocks with fewer than 20 IEX trades that day.
  • Per board: negative equity is excluded from P/B; loss-making companies are excluded from P/E.

4. Checks before release

  1. The No.1–No.3 rows are compared with the company's original 10-Q or 10-K by the operator.
  2. Every number on screen is compared automatically with the source table; any mismatch stops the upload.
  3. Week-over-week changes ("3 new · 3 out") are computed from last week's archived table, so each board shows what actually changed.

5. What a board can't tell you

  • Whether the company will earn more or less next year. All inputs are past results.
  • One-off items. A single large gain can make P/E or FCF yield look cheap for a year.
  • Industry differences. Energy and refining often rank high on P/E and FCF yield late in a profit cycle.
  • Official closing prices. We show IEX's last sale, which for large companies is usually within about 0.1% of the official close, but is not the same thing.
  • Different fiscal calendars. Quarter ends differ by company (for example Sep 26 vs Sep 30), so balance-sheet dates are shown per row.

6. How this was made

  • Who: 7 Days Report is run by a small independent team that reviews every video before release. We have no business relationship with the companies shown and take no payment to include or exclude any company.
  • Software: the data is collected and ranked by our own scripts from the public sources above. Videos are rendered from templates; music is our own composition.
  • Review: a person checks every number, title and video against the sources before release.
